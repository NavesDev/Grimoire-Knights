# Models

3D models (GeckoLib) generated by script inside Blockbench. The script is the source; everything else is generated.

## Where things live
| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `art/models/<model>.js` | Source: bones, cubes, materials and texture painting |
| `art/models/lib.js` | Assembly, UV packing and shared painting |
| `art/models/run.js` | Model list and generation |
| `art/blockbench/<model>.bbmodel` | Generated; versioned to open and view in Blockbench |
| `assets/grimoireknights/geo/<feature>/` | Generated; geometry used by the game |
| `assets/grimoireknights/textures/{entity,block}/<feature>/` | Generated; textures and variants |
| `assets/grimoireknights/animations/<feature>/` | Generated; animations used by the game |

## Rules
- Model changes go in the `.js`. Hand edits to `.geo.json`, `.png` or `.bbmodel` are lost on the next generation.
- A new variant (element, rank) is a new palette in the model's `.js`.
- New model: create `art/models/<model>.js` and add it to `MODELS` in `run.js`.
- Every model uses 2 texture pixels per unit (`DENSITY` in `run.js`); a cube can multiply that with `res` when its shape is cut out in the texture (wings, headband and chest mark of the demon form).
- New animations or animation tweaks also go in the `.js` (`animations` field); animations made by hand in Blockbench are lost on the next generation.
- Generation is deterministic for geo, textures and animations; the `.bbmodel` only changes in UUIDs.
- One agent at a time in Blockbench: generation opens and closes projects in the app.

## How to generate
With Blockbench open and the Blockbench MCP server on, run through `risky_eval` (an empty list generates all):

```js
(()=>{const fs=require('fs'),path=require('path');const cache={};const load=f=>{if(cache[f])return cache[f];const m={exports:{}};new Function('module','exports','load','fs','path','__dirname',fs.readFileSync(f,'utf8'))(m,m.exports,p=>load(path.resolve(path.dirname(f),p)),fs,path,path.dirname(f));return cache[f]=m.exports;};return load('/home/naves/Projetos/Grimoire-Knights/art/models/run.js')(['grimoire'])})()
```

Blockbench doesn't load files with `require`; the snippet above reads each `.js` and hands it `load`, `fs`, `path` and `__dirname`.

## Conventions the code needs to know
- **Grimoire:** bones `front_cover`, `back_cover` and `flip_page` rotate on the Y axis around the spine to open. `sword_anchor` marks where the anti-magic sword comes out. Textures `grimoire_<element>_<leaves>`.
- **Mantle and banner:** two textures drawn one over the other. `_cloth` is light gray, tinted with the squad color; `_trim` has the border, wood, gold and the crest plate. The mantle's `tail` bone shows from Knight on.
- **Mantle:** open front (two flaps and a collar with a clasp), crest plate on the left flap. The black squad uses dark dye on `_cloth`.
- **Demon form:** a layer over the player. Bones `s1_` to `s4_` are the stages (arm, half body, full form, union); the renderer shows all of them up to the current stage, and a hidden bone hides its children. Stages 1 to 3 are black only (anti-magic): 1 right arm and tentacles on the chest; 2 right horn and small wing; 3 left arm with its tentacles and both full wings (the small one stays hidden inside the right one), torso and legs still showing. Stage 4 (union) covers torso and legs and adds the left horn, black headband with the red mark, fixed red marks (chest; spiked stripe on the front and back of the arms and on the shin), pauldrons with a raised red star, claws and tail. The player's face stays visible. Wings (dark black, torn flame) and the headband front are planes with the shape cut out in the texture; wings, tentacles, marks and tail tip use transparency: render with the cutout render type.
- **Renegade captain:** same skeleton as the mage; the bigger size comes from the renderer's scale.
- **Anchors:** `grimoire_anchor` (mages) and `book_anchor` (altar) mark where the grimoire floats.

## Animations
`animations` field in the model's `.js`; `K.animate` (`lib.js`) creates them in the project and `run.js` writes the `.animation.json`.

```js
animations: {
  float: { loop: 'loop', length: 3, bones: { root: { position: [0, 'math.sin(query.anim_time * 120) * 0.6', 0] } } },
  open: { loop: 'hold', length: 0.5, bones: { front_cover: { rotation: [[0, [0, 0, 0]], [0.5, [0, -75, 0], 'catmullrom']] } } }
}
```

- In-game name: `animation.<model>.<name>` (e.g. `animation.grimoire.open`).
- `loop`: `loop`, `hold` (stops on the last frame) or `once`.
- Channel: a list of `[time, [x, y, z], interpolation]` (default `linear`), or a single `[x, y, z]`, constant or Molang. Continuous loops work better in Molang with `query.anim_time`.
- Rotation in the Blockbench convention; the export flips it for GeckoLib.
- Avoid two controllers moving the same bone at the same time: the sway stays on `root` and the rest on the bones below.
- Check in Blockbench: Animate mode, pick the animation and move the timeline.

### Grimoire
| Animation | Loop | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `float` | loop | Floats beside the player (`root`), own controller |
| `open` / `close` | hold / once | Opens and closes the covers |
| `flip` | once | Turns a page with the book open, when casting |
| `draw` | once | Anti-magic: moves from the player's left to in front of the right hand, lies down, opens and flips while the sword comes out at `sword_anchor` (grabbed around 1.05 s), then closes and goes back |
| `altar` | once | At the Grimoire Altar, together with the altar's `activate` (1.5 s): comes down to the lectern while lying down and opening, flips forward, back and forward, rises and closes |
| `summon` | hold | Ceremony: grows slowly at the bookshelves (1 s, code holds 20 ticks) before flying to the player (80 ticks) |
| `choose` | once | Ceremony: spins down to the player; then closes and vanishes with `vanish` (the HUD shows the grimoire from then on) |

The grimoire entity stays still at `FLOAT_AT` (`grimoire.js`): to the player's left, away from the sword hand, facing where they look. `draw` carries the book from there to the hand through the `book` bone (position), without moving the entity; `float` keeps running on `root`. The player's pose reaching out and drawing the sword goes in playerAnimator, synced with `draw`.

At the altar, code carries the grimoire from the player's side to `ALTAR_LIFT` (6) above the altar's `book_anchor`, in a ~0.6 s arc, turning to face the reader and tilting like the lectern (-15°); the flight there and back stays in code because the distance varies. There it plays `altar` together with the altar's `activate`: the book comes down to the lectern while lying down and rises before standing up, without passing through the lectern. Then it arcs back to the player's side.

### Demon form
Body, head, arms and legs follow the player's pose in the renderer; the animations only move the extras. `idle` and `fly_*` only rotate; `awaken`, `revert` and `transform_<n>` only scale, so they can play on separate controllers.

Skin (arm plates, tentacles, chest, union marks, legs; `SPREAD` in `demon_form.js`) doesn't scale: the anti-magic spreads through the texture. Each texel has the time it turns, from the distance to where the piece starts (hand, shoulders, hip) plus patchy noise and early specks; it shows blood red, darkens and settles on the final color. Textures are generated per frame in `textures/entity/awakening/demon_form_spread/` (`awaken_NN`, `transform_1_NN`, `transform_3_NN`, `transform_4_NN`, 8 per second, `FPS`); the renderer swaps the texture by the animation time and, on `revert`, uses `awaken_NN` backwards. Outside these animations it uses `demon_form.png`.

Horns, wings, crown and tail grow as pieces, in bursts with their own timings: horns and tail segment by segment, wings are born folded and open past the mark. The whole transformation is 25% slower (`PACE`) so the spread can be seen.

| Animation | Loop | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `idle` | loop | Wings breathing, tail swaying |
| `awaken` | once | Awakening on: the stages spread in order; those above the player's stage stay hidden |
| `revert` | once | Awakening over: the same in reverse, from the last stage to the first |
| `transform_1` to `transform_4` | once | New stage (demon quest): only that stage spreads |
| `fly_hover`, `fly_forward`, `fly_backward`, `fly_left`, `fly_right` | loop | Wing beat for each flight direction (from stage 2 on), together with the player's pose |
| `fly_dash` | loop | Dash: wings swept back, trembling fast, tail stretched |

## Player poses (playerAnimator)
Rig `player_sword.js` (`kind: 'player'`): `run.js` writes only the `.bbmodel` and `assets/grimoireknights/player_animation/player_sword.json`, with no geo or texture.

- playerAnimator reads the GeckoLib format, but without Molang: loops become numeric keyframes (`loop` in `player_sword.js`, 12 per cycle).
- Bones: `body` (the whole player), `head`, `torso`, `right_arm`, `left_arm`, `right_leg`, `left_leg`, siblings as in the vanilla player.
- A part without keyframes returns to the default pose, not the vanilla one; that's why walking and running animate legs and arms. `head` has no keys so it follows the look (check in game).
- Sword in hand: blade forward with the edge down (katana with the curve up), same as the end of the draw. The held item renderer sets the orientation; the poses don't rotate the sword.
- Not yet tested in game: axis signs and whether the keyless `head` follows the camera.

| Animation | Loop | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `walk` | loop 1 s | Walking with a sword: blade forward, low; free arm and legs in step |
| `run` | loop 0.6 s | Running: torso leaning, sword on the shoulder with the blade down the back, free arm pumping |

Rig `player_demon.js`: flight in demon form, each pose with the `fly_<dir>` of the same length.

| Animation | Loop | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `hover` | loop 1 s | Still in the air: loose legs, rises and falls with each beat |
| `forward` | loop 0.8 s | Leaning forward, legs back, arms swept, head looking ahead |
| `backward` | loop 1 s | Leaning back, arms and legs forward, braking |
| `left` / `right` | loop 0.8 s | Leans into the turn, outer arm open |
| `dash` | loop 0.4 s | Dash: body flat, right fist forward, legs together |
| `awaken` | once 4.1 s | Together with the demon form's `awaken`: hunches gripping the arm, trembles, opens the arms when the wings burst, hunches again at the union and stands up |
| `revert` | once 4.1 s | Together with `revert`: the power drains, gets exhausted and recovers at the end |

Rig `player_ride.js`: standing flight, like a hoverboard, on the broom and the greatsword. Body sideways on the board (right foot toward the nose, `body` y -90), head turned where it goes, arms open along the board. The renderer stands the player on the broom or the laid-down sword and tilts both while maneuvering.

- With the body turned 90°, the `body`'s `x` and `z` rotate on the same axis (gimbal lock): the tilt is built on world axes and converted to ZYX Euler in `player_ride.js`.
- With no knee, leaning forward or back lowers a hip: each leg opens along the board just enough for both feet (by the lowest corner) to stay on the board's floor.
- Each pose rises, falls and rolls together with the prop animation of the same length; checked in Blockbench with the feet within 0.1 of the board.
- Not yet tested in game: whether playerAnimator applies `body` in the same ZYX order.

| Animation | Loop | Pair | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `broom_hover`, `sword_hover` | 2 s | broom `hover` / greatsword `ride` | Still in the air, standing |
| `broom_forward`, `sword_forward` | 0.5 s / 2 s | broom `fly` / greatsword `ride` | Leans toward the nose, back arm high |
| `broom_backward`, `sword_backward` | 0.5 s / 2 s | same | Leans toward the tail, braking, front arm high |
| `broom_left`, `sword_left` | 0.5 s / 2 s | same | Leans on the heels, arms forward, looks into the turn |
| `broom_right`, `sword_right` | 0.5 s / 2 s | same | Leans on the toes, arms back, looks into the turn |
